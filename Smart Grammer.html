Parts of Speech

Parts of Speech are the basic building blocks of English grammar.They classify words according to the role the play in a sentence.Every word in English belongs to one of the *eight* parts of speech,and each has a spesific function that helps us express ideas clearly and correctly.

1.Nouns

A noun is the name of person,place,animal or anything is called Noun.

Example:

  • Ali is my friend.

Types of Noun(5)

  • Proper Noun: The specific name of person,place,or thing.it always begin with a capital latters.
    Example:Ali,Pakistan,Lahore,Monday,etc.
  • Common Noun: The general name of a person,place or thing.
    Example:Boy,City,School,Book,etc.
  • Collective Noun: A word that refers to a group of peaple,animals or things.
    Example:team,class,army,flock,etc.
  • Abstract Noun: The name of an idea,quality,feeling,or state that cannot be seen or touch.
    Example:honesty,happiness,bravery,love,etc.
  • Material Noun: The name of material or substance from which things are made.
    Example:gold,water,wood,etc.

2.Pronoun

A pronoun replaces the noun. repeating the same noun.

Example:

Ali is a student.He is very intelligent.

Here,He is used instead of repeating the nounAli

Types of pronoun(8)

  • Personal Pronoun: Replaces a persons name.
    Example:I,you,he,she,it,they.
  • Possessive Pronoun: Shows ownership.
    Example:mine,yours,his,hers,ours,theirs.
  • Reflexive Pronoun: Refers back to the subject.
    Example:myself,yourself,himself, herself,ourselves,themselves.
  • Demonstrative Pronoun: Points to specific things.
    Example:this,that,these,those.
  • Introgetive Pronoun: Ask questions.
    Example:who,whom,whose,which,what.
  • Relative Pronoun: Joins clauses.
    Example:who,whom,whose,which,what.
  • Indefinite Pronoun: Refers to non-specific peoples or things.
    Example:someone,anyone,everyone,no-one,nobody,each,few.
  • Distributive Pronoun: Refers to members of a group one at a time.
    Example:each,either,neither.

In Direct Speech: exact words quetation marks (" ") write with speaker. In Indirect Speech: speakers words (dialogues) repoted.

In Indirect Speech any person words (dialogues) reported.

Example:

Ali said that he was happy.

  • Reported Speech: Actual words of speaker.

Example:

Ali said "I am busy."

Modal Verbs

Modal verbs are helping verbs that are used with a main verb to express ability, possibility, permission, necessity, advise, obligation, intension, or willingness.

Modal verbs are followed by the base form of the main verb.

Basic Structure

Subject + Modal verb + Base verb + Object

Example: She can swim.

1. Can

Can is used to express ability, possibility, and informal permission.

Ability

Example: Ali can speak English.

Permission

Example: Can I use your pen?

Possibility

Example: It can be very cold here in winter.

Negative

Example: She cannot swim.

2. Could

Could is used to express past ability, polite requests, and possibility.

Past Ability

Example: When I was young, I could run fast.

Polite Request

Example: Could you help me?

Possibility

Example: It could rain tonight.

Negative

Example: He could not attend the meeting.

3. May

May is used to express possibility and formal permission.

Possibility

Example: It may rain today.

Permission

Example: He may not come today.

4. Might

Might is used to express a possibility that is less certain.

Possibility

Example: She might come to the party.

Negative

Example: He might not come tomarrow.

5. Must

Must is used to express strong necessity, obligation, or a strong conclusion.

Necessity

Example: You must obey the rules.

Strong Conclusion

Example: She must be tired after the long journey.

Negative

Example: You must not touch this machine.

6. Should

Should is mainly used to give advice, make recommendation, or express expectation.

Advice

Example: You should study regularly.

Recommendation

Example: You should drink enough water.

Expectation

Example: The bus should arrive soon.

Negative

Example: You should not waste your time.

7. Will

Will is used to express future actions, decisions, promises, and willingness.

Future Action

Example: I will visit your home tomarrow.

Promise

Example: I will help you.

Willingness

Example: I will carry your bag.

Negative

Example: I will not forget you.

8. Would

Would is used for polite requests, preferences, hypothetical situations, and reported speech.

Polite Request

Example: Would you help me?

Preference

Example: I would rether stay at home.

Hypothetical Situation

Example: I would travel the world if I had enough money.

Reported Speech

Example: He said that he would help me.

9. Shall

Shall is mainly used with I and we for seggestions, offers, and formal future expression.

Suggestion

Example: Shall we go for a walk?

Offer

Example: Shall I open the window?

10. Ought to

Ought to is used to express advice, duty, or something that is normally right.

Advice

Example: You ought to respect your parents.

Duty

Example: We ought to help peaple in need.

Negative

Example: You ought not to waste food.

11. Need

Need can be used as a modal verb to express necessity, especially in negative sentences and questions.

Necessity

Example: Need I say more?

Negative

Example: You need not worry.

In everyday English, need is also commonly used as a normal verb:

Example: I need study.

12. Dare

Dare can be used as a modal verb to express courage or challenge.

Courage or Challenge

Example: Dare you speak to me like that?

Negative

Example: He dare not speak against his teacher.

In everyday English dare is also commonly used as a normal verb:

Example: I don't dare to ask him.

13. Used to

Used to is used to talk about past habits or past situations that are no longer true.

Past Habit

Example: I used to play cricket everyday.

Past Situation

Example: There used to be a park here.

Negative

Example: I did not used to play cricket.

14. Basic Structure of Modal Verbs

Modal verbs are followed by the base form of the main verb.

Affirmative

Subject + Modal + Base Verb + Object

Example: She can speak English.

Negative

Subject + Modal + not + Base Verb + Object

Example: She cannot speak English.

Interrogative

Modal + Subject + Base Verb + Object?

Example: Can she speak English?

15. Important Rules of Modal Verbs

  • A modal verb is followed by the base form of a verb.
  • Do not add -s or -es to the main verb after a modal.
  • Modal verbs do not normally use do, does, or did to form questions or negatives.
  • The modal verb remains the same with different subjects.

Correct:

She can play cricket.

Incorrect:

She can plays cricket.

Prepositions

A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word in a sentence. Prepositions can show place, time, direction, movement, position, and other relationships.

Basic Structure

Preposition + Noun / Pronoun

Example: The book is on the table.

1. Prepositions of Time

Prepositions of time are used to show when something happens or happened.

In

In is used with months, years, seasons, centuries, and longer periods of time.

Examples:

  • I was born in 2010.
  • We will travel in December.
  • Flowers bloom in spring.

On

On is used with days and dates.

Examples:

  • I will visit your home on Monday.
  • My exam is on 15th August.
  • We met on Sunday morning.

At

At is used with specific times and certain fixed expressions of time.

Examples:

  • The class starts at 8 o'clock.
  • I wake up at 6 a.m.
  • We usually sleep at night.

Before

Before means earlier than a particular time or event.

Example: Wash your hands before dinner.

After

After means later than a perticular time or event.

Example: We went home after school.

During

During means at some time within a period or event.

Example: Do not talk during the lacture.

Since

Since is used to show the starting point of an action or situation that continues to present.

Example: I have lived here since 2020.

For

For is used to show the duration af an action or situation.

Example: She has studied English for three years.

Untill / Till

Untill / Till shows the time up to which something continues.

Example: I waited untill 5 o'clock.

2. Prepositions of Place

Prepositions of place show where a person, animal, or thing is located.

In

In is used when something is inside a place, area, or container.

Example: The keys are in in the beg.

On

On is used when something is touching or positioned on a surface.

Example: The book is on the table.

At

At is used for a particular point or location.

Example: She is waiting at the bus stop.

Under

Under means below something.

Example: The cat is under the table.

Over

Over means above something often directly above it.

Example: The lamp is over the table.

Above

Above means at a higher position than something.

Example: The picture is above the sofa.

Below

Below mean at a lower position than something.

Example: The temperature is below zero.

Behind

Behind mean at or towards the back of something.

Example: The boy is standing behind the door.

In front of

In front of means a head of something or someone.

Example: The car is in front of the house.

Beside

Beside means next to or at the side of something or soemone.

Example: She sat beside her mother.

Between

Between is commonly used when something is in the space separating two people or thing.

Example: The school is between the bank of the park.

Among

Among is used when something is surrounded by or included in a group of more than two.

Example: She was sitting among her friends.

3. Preposition of movement and direction

These prepositions show movement from one place to another.

To

To shows movement towards a place or person.

Example: He went to school.

From

From shows the starting point or movement.

Example: She came from Lahore.

Into

Into shows movement from outside to inside.

A conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases, or clauses in a sentence.

Conjunctions help us to connect ideas and make sentences clear meaningful.

Example:

1. Types of Conjunctions

There are three main types of conjunctions:

2. Coordinating Conjunctions

Coordinating conjunctions connects word, phrases, or independent clauses that have equal importance.

The seven main Coordinating Conjunctions are commonly remembered by the word FANBOYS.

FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

For

For is used to give a reason or explanation.

Example: I stayed at home, for it was raining.

And

Examples:

  • Ali and Ahmad are brothers.
  • She bought a book and a pen.

Nor

Nor is used to connect two negative ideas.

Example: He does not eat meat, nor does he drink milk.

But

But is used to show contrast between ideas.

Example: I wanted to play, but it was raining.

Or

Or is used to show a choice or alternative.

Example: Would you like tea, or coffee?

Yet

Yet is used to show contrast, similar to 'but'

Example: He is young yet he is very responsible.

So

So is used to show a result or consequence.

Example: It was raining, so we stayed at home.

3.Subordinating Conjunctions

Subordinating conjunctions connect a dependent clause with an independent clause.

They can show the relationships such as reason, time, condition, contrast, purpose, and result.

Common Subordinating Conjunctions

Example: She was reading while I was studying.

Before

Example: Wash your hands before you eat.

After

Example: We went home after tha class ended.

Time Example: I have lived here since 2020.

Example: wait here untill I come back.

As can be used to show time, reason, or manner depending on the sentence.

Example: As I was leaving, the phone rang.

So That

So that is used to express purpose.

Example: He studied hard so that he could pass tha exam.

4. Correlative Conjunctions

Correlative conjunctions are pairs of words that work together to connect words, phrases, or clauses.

Common pairs include:

  • Either...or
  • Neither...nor
  • Both...and
  • Not only...but also
  • Whether...or

Either...or

Either...or is used to show a choice between two possibilities.

Example: You can either stay here or come with us.

Neither...nor

Neither...nor is used to connect two negative alternatives.

Example: Neither Ali nor Ahmad was present.

Both...and

Both...and is used to connect two positive or related ideas.

Example: Both Ali and Ahmad are good students.

Not only...but also

Not only...but also is used to emphasize two related ideas.

Example: She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

Whether...or

Whether...or is used when there are two possible alternatives.

Example: I don't know whether he will come or stay home.

5. Conjunctions Showing Purpose

Some conjunctions are used to explain the purpose of an action.

So That

Example: She studied hard so that she could pass.

In Order That

In order that is used to express the purpose.

Example: He worked hard in order that his family could have a better life.

  • Because
  • Since
  • As

Example: We stayed inside because it was raining.

7. Conjunctions Showing Contrast

These conjunctions connect ideas that are different or opposite.

  • But
  • Although
  • Though
  • Even though

Articles are words that are used before nouns to show whether, we are talking about something specific or general.

1. Article "A"

A is an indefinite article. It is used before a singular countable noun whenwe are talking about one person, place, animal, or thing in a general or non-specific way.

Basic Rule

A + Singular Countable Noun

  • I saw a bird.
  • He is a student.
  • She bought a book.
  • Ali has a bicycle.

Use a before a word that begins with a consonant sound.

  • a boy.
  • a car.
  • a house.
  • a university.
  • a European country.

Notice that university and European begin with vowel letters. but their pronounciation starts with a constant sound.

An is an indefinite article. It is used before a singular countable noun when the following word begins with a vowel sound.

Basic Rule

An + Singular Countable Noun

Examples:

  • I ate an apple.
  • She has an umbrella.
  • He is an honest man.
  • We saw an elephant.

Before a vowel sound

Use an before a word that begins with a vowel sound.

  • an apple.
  • an egg.
  • an orange.
  • an hour.
  • an honest person.

The important point is that we choose a or an according to the sound, not simply the first letter.

Degrees of comparison are used to compare the qualities of peaple, animals, places, or things.

If + Present Simple, will + Base Verb

If + Past Simple, would + Base Verb

  • If I had more time, I would learm another language.
  • If I were rich, I would help others.
  • If she studied harder, she would get better result.

4. Third Conditional

The third conditional is used to talk about an unreal or imagined situation in the past and its possible past result.

Structure:

If + Past Perfect, would have + Past Participle

Examples:

  • If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.
  • If she had left earlier, she would have caught the bus.
  • If they had worked hard, they would have succeeded.

5. Conditional with "Unless"

Unless means "if not". If is used to express a negative condition.

Examples:

  • You will fail unless you study.
  • We will not go outside unless it stops raining.

An idiom is a group of words that has a special meaning, different from the literal meaning of the individual words.

In simple words, an idiom cannot usually be understood by translating each word separately.

  • A piece of cake - Something is very easy.
